Words used in this manual

Overhead tankThe tank on the roof. The controller fills it.
SumpThe tank at ground level or under the ground. The motor takes water from it.
Bore motor 2 motorsThe motor in the borewell. It fills the overhead tank when the sump is empty.
Float switchA switch in the tank that tells the controller "full" or "not full".
Tank unit WirelessA small solar-powered box on the tank. It sends the level to the controller by radio.
TripThe controller stops the motor because of a fault, to protect it.

Safety first

The controller works with 230 V mains. Only a trained electrician should install it, open it or change its wiring.
  • Switch off the mains before you touch any wire.
  • The motor must have its own MCB (circuit breaker) and a good earth connection.
  • Keep the controller indoors, away from water, rain and direct sun.
  • Do not block the air holes of the box.
  • If you see smoke, smell burning or hear clicking that does not stop: switch off the mains and call us.

Auto mode: how it works

In Auto mode you do nothing. The controller watches the tanks and runs the motor.

1 motor 1 motor

  1. The overhead tank is full. The motor is off. Line 4: OVER HEAD TANK FULL.
  2. People use water. The level goes down. The float shows OF:LOW.
  3. The controller beeps 5 times, then one long beep. Line 4 counts down: STARTING MOTOR 5 … 1. This warns people near the motor.
  4. The motor starts. Line 4: FILLING TANK. The MOTOR ON light is on.
  5. The tank becomes full. The motor stops by itself.

If the sump is empty, the motor does not start. Line 4 shows SUMP LOW - WAITING. When water comes back into the sump, the motor starts again by itself.

2 motors: sump motor first, then bore motor 2 motors

  1. The overhead tank is full. Both motors are off.
  2. People use water. The level goes down.
  3. 5 beeps, then the sump motor starts. Line 4: FILLING: SUMP MOTOR.
  4. If the sump becomes empty, the sump motor stops. 5 beeps, then the bore motor starts. Line 4: FILLING: BORE MOTOR.
  5. The bore motor keeps filling until the overhead tank is full, even if water comes back into the sump.
  6. The tank is full. All motors stop. Next time, the sump motor runs first again.
The bore motor also takes over when the sump motor has tripped (for example an overload). The water supply does not stop because one motor has a problem.

Only one motor runs at a time. The controller never switches on both motors together.

Wireless models Wireless

The tank units send the level every 3 to 4 seconds. When a level changes, they send it at once. If the controller hears nothing from a tank unit for 12 seconds, it stops the motor to be safe and shows LoRa: OH TANK LOST or LoRa: SUMP LOST.

The controller measures the voltage and the motor current all the time. It protects the motor in Auto mode, Manual mode and Motor test.

Voltage: an example

High voltage limit 270 V, restart gap 8 V. At 9:00 the voltage goes to 275 V. After 5 s the motor stops. At 9:20 the voltage is 265 V: still too close to the limit, so the motor waits. At 9:30 the voltage is 255 V (below 270 − 8 = 262 V). The restart delay of 60 s runs. At 9:31 the motor may start again.

Dry run: step by step

  1. The motor runs, but the current is too low. The countdown DT:05 shows on line 3.
  2. After 5 s the motor stops. Line 4: DRY RETRY 1 WAIT 25s.
  3. After 30 s it tries again. This can happen 3 times.
  4. If there is still no water: DRY RUN TRIP. The FAULT light comes on and the buzzer beeps for 10 s.
  5. In Auto mode the controller tries again by itself after 30 minutes. Line 4 shows the time left: DRY RUN TRIP R:25m. It does this up to 3 times. After the 3rd time, you must do a fault reset.
This is useful for a borewell: when the water comes back into the well, the controller starts again without you.

Fault reset

Overload, dry run and maximum run time trips keep the motor off until you reset them. This makes you check the problem first.

  1. Read the message on line 4 (it shows SMP or BOR: which motor tripped).
  2. Find and fix the cause. Examples: closed valve, no water, blocked pipe, burst pipe, jammed pump.
  3. Hold ENTER for 5 seconds. The display shows FAULT RESET DONE.
If the same trip comes back again and again, do not keep resetting. Call your technician. The motor may be damaged.

Wireless tank units Wireless

A tank unit is a small box on the tank. It has a float switch, a solar panel, a battery and a radio. It sends the tank level to the controller.

  • The solar panel charges the battery. Keep the panel clean and in the sun, facing south if possible.
  • The battery voltage shows on the wireless page (for example 4.1V). Below 3.5 V the controller shows TANK UNIT BATT LOW.
  • The unit sends a message every 3 to 4 seconds, and at once when the level changes.

If a tank unit is lost

  1. Look at the wireless page. LOST = it was working before. NOT SEEN = no message since power-on.
  2. Check the solar panel and the battery voltage.
  3. Check that nothing new blocks the radio path, for example a new metal sheet or a new wall.
  4. If it does not come back, call service.
When a tank unit is lost, the motor stops. This is safe: the controller never runs the motor without knowing the tank level.

Solenoid valve Option

Some installations have a 230 V solenoid valve (an electric water valve) in the pipe. The controller works it automatically:

  1. After the 5 beeps, the valve opens first. Line 4 shows STARTING MOTOR VLV.
  2. 3 seconds later the motor starts (setting VALVE OPEN DELAY).
  3. When the motor stops, the valve closes 3 seconds later.

The model name ends with -V when the valve option is fitted, for example DJ-W2-V.

Care and FAQ

Care

  • Clean the float switches when you clean the tanks.
  • Wipe the controller with a dry cloth only.
  • Clean the solar panels every few months.
